01 / Find the blind spots

High activity does not always mean high impact.

Most gaps hide inside programs that are busy, well-attended and regularly reported as “on track.” Learning analytics should question surface-level success and turn hidden patterns into signals for action.

◒

Identify low-impact programs

Look for high activity with little outcome—not simply low participation. Compare effort against results instead of reviewing activity in isolation.

Signals to look forConsistently high completion with no improvement in assessments or skills; strong engagement followed by no change in on-the-job metrics; repeated relaunches without improved outcomes.
CompletionSkill movementWork metrics
⌁

Spot skill & performance gaps

Skill gaps rarely appear as missing learning. They appear as stalled progress. Compare where learning effort is concentrated with where performance breaks down.

Signals to look forTraining completed but skill level unchanged; slower progression by role/team despite equal access; persistent performance issues after repeated interventions.
Role differencesAssessment trendsPractice
↗

Use trends—not snapshots

Track patterns across months to see whether impact is sustained, improving or decaying.

Signals to look forSkills that improve then plateau; engagement that drops after launch; performance gains that fade when support is removed.
Monthly trendPlateauDecay
The diagnostic shift
Move from “Do we need more training?” to “Are we targeting the right capability?”

The gap may not be the volume of learning. It may be relevance, sequencing, or lack of practice in real work contexts. Programs may be outdated, misaligned with current work, or solving a problem that no longer exists.

02 / Metric map

Seven signals that connect learning to impact.

No single metric tells the full story. Together, these indicators move from visibility to capability and business outcomes.

↗

Enrollment rate

Shows whether learning is seen as relevant—an indicator of demand, alignment and timing.

ExampleA leadership program launched during peak review cycles has low enrollment despite strong executive sponsorship. Timing may be the issue, not interest.
✓

Completion rate

Shows whether learners can follow through. Completion alone is neutral; context matters across mandatory, optional and long-form programs.

ExampleAn optional manager program has 40% completion while mandatory compliance training is at 90%. This may signal perceived value, not effectiveness.
◎

Engagement signals

Track repeat visits, content saves/bookmarks, practice attempts and time spent on applied activities.

ExampleLearners return to role-based simulations but skip long conceptual videos. Engagement suggests what holds attention—not proof of impact.
⌁

Assessment & skill progression

Compare pre/post-learning results and skill movement over time rather than relying on one-time scores.

ExampleSkill proficiency rises one level within six weeks of applied learning, while quiz scores remain largely unchanged.
⇢

Knowledge-to-performance conversion

Look for changed behavior and better execution: task accuracy, faster completion and more consistent on-job application.

ExampleError rates fall after task-based training even though completion rates stay flat. This is a bridge between learning and ROI.
♧

Retention & internal mobility

Track whether learners stay longer, move into new roles and build capabilities aligned to internal demand.

ExampleEmployees who complete skill pathways show higher internal movement within nine months.
▥

Business impact indicators

Connect learning to sales performance, quality/error rates, speed/productivity, customer satisfaction and resolution outcomes.

ExampleTeams completing product enablement close deals faster than teams that did not. Show contribution over time; do not claim learning caused the result alone.
03 / Analytics workflow

Turn signals into decisions, not another report.

Investigate gaps, validate the cause and improve the learning experience.

Spot the signal

Find mismatches: high completion / flat skills, low enrollment by role, or fading performance gains.

Segment the data

Compare roles, teams, regions, pathways and time periods.

Investigate why

Talk to learners and managers. Test timing, relevance, sequencing, access or practice barriers.

Adapt the program

Adjust format, targeting, timing, applied practice or manager support.

Measure again

Track enrollment, skill movement and on-job performance; check whether improvement lasts.

1

Activity

Who enrolled, attended, completed or returned?

2

Capability

What changed in assessment, proficiency and applied practice?

3

Performance

What shifted at work, and is the change sustained?

04 / Case study

From completion data to a better learner experience.

A practical example of how a learning analytics signal can lead to action.

Portfolio case study
The situation

Managers weren’t enrolling.

Through in-depth learning analytics research, Clara, an L&D manager, found that employees at the managerial level were not enrolling in courses.

✓Read the signal: Enrollment patterns revealed a participation gap in a specific learner group.
✓Ask, don’t assume: Clara held a session with managers to understand their learning experience preferences and aspirations.
✓Adapt the experience: She tweaked the learning programs in response.
✓Re-measure: Enrollment improved after the program changes.
The lesson

Analytics is the start of a conversation.

Data highlighted the gap; listening helped explain it; a targeted change created a better fit.

Signal →
Insight →
Action

No uplift percentage is stated because the source describes a better enrollment rate but does not specify a figure.

Executive takeaway
Learning analytics becomes powerful when it challenges what looks successful on the surface.

Use activity metrics to find the signal, skill and performance data to investigate the gap, learner conversations to understand the cause, and follow-up measurement to see whether the action worked. Treat correlation as evidence of contribution—not automatic proof of causation.
